# Phase 3 — Evaluation

This covers the evaluation loop (`evaluate_phase3.py`) built on top of Phase 2's
`ask.py`: a 20-question test set with ground truth verified against the real
corpus, two real failures found and root-caused during actual runs (not
hypothetical), and a final clean result.

## 1. Building the evaluation script (`evaluate_phase3.py`)

Rather than reimplementing retrieval/generation/citation logic, the script
imports `load_resources()` and `ask()` directly from `ask.py` and runs them
against a fixed test set, scoring four things per question:

- **Retrieval hit** — was the expected article among the chunks Chroma
  actually returned?
- **Citation accuracy** — did the model cite the expected article(s), out of
  what it cited?
- **Hallucination count** — reuses `ask.py`'s own cited-vs-retrieved
  cross-check rather than recomputing it.
- **Abstention correctness** — checked in both directions: did it wrongly
  abstain on an answerable question, and did it wrongly answer an
  out-of-scope one?

Reuses the same resilience pattern from Phase 2's `ask.py main()`:
per-question `try/except`, incremental disk writes after every question, so
a batch of 20 real API calls surviving a mid-run failure was a design
requirement, not an afterthought.

## 2. Building real ground truth (not from memory)

The first draft of the test set had `expected_articles` filled in from
general knowledge of Egyptian Labor Law No. 14 of 2025. Once given the
actual `labor_law_articles.json`, every value was checked directly against
the corpus text — the same standard Phase 2 held the model to
("verifying each expected answer directly against the specific article
text, not from memory or general legal knowledge").

That check caught three questions that didn't actually belong in the set:

- **Work-injury compensation** — Article 1's definitions clause explicitly
  defers `إصابة العمل` to the Social Insurance Law (148/2019) rather than
  defining it in this law. There's no article to check the system against.
- **Iddah/widowhood leave** — no such provision exists anywhere in the
  corpus.
- **Trade union multi-membership** — not addressed by this law (Trade Union
  Law 213/2017 territory).

Two more were reworded once the real text was checked, rather than dropped:

- "Fixed-term contract maximum duration" -> this law doesn't cap fixed-term
  length outright; reworded to the provision that does exist (Article 154's
  right to end a >5-year contract without compensation).
- "End-of-service gratuity" -> general gratuity was mostly replaced by the
  pension system; reworded to the specific provision that survives it
  (Article 172's gratuity for service continued past age 60).

**Final set:** 15 in-scope questions, each mapped to a single
corpus-verified article, spread across topics not already covered by Phase
1/2's smoke-test questions (probation, working hours, maternity leave,
annual leave already covered there — Phase 3 added notice periods, child
labor protections, rest-day pay, dismissal grounds, disciplinary sanctions,
sick leave, contract termination rules, and nursing breaks). Plus 5
out-of-scope questions (criminal law, tax law, property law, family law,
traffic law) to test abstention.

## 3. Retrieval failure: the Article 156 notice-period miss

**Problem found:** on the first full run, "ما هي مدة مهلة الإخطار بإنهاء عقد
العمل غير محدد المدة؟" (notice period duration) retrieved articles 88, 154,
157, 164, and 241 at `top_k=5` — never Article 156, the one article that
actually states the duration (three months).

**Root cause:** Article 156 states the duration itself
(`قبل الإنهاء بثلاثة أشهر`) but never uses the phrase `مهلة الإخطار`.
Articles 158-164 use that exact phrase repeatedly — they govern what
happens *during* the notice period (when it starts, what pauses it, what
the parties owe each other) — but none of them state its length. The
question's own phrasing lexically/semantically matched the procedural
neighbors far more strongly than the article that actually answers it.

A diagnostic query with `n_results=30` confirmed this wasn't a deep
semantic miss: Article 156 ranked 8th, with distances tightly clustered
between ranks 5 and 8 (0.1547 to 0.1605) — a narrow, borderline miss
caused specifically by `top_k=5` being too tight for this case, not a
broken embedding.

**Fix:** raised `TOP_K` from 5 to 8 in `ask.py`. Re-running the full
20-question set confirmed the fix worked — Article 156 was retrieved and
correctly cited — with no regressions on any of the other 19 questions
(retrieval hit rate and citation accuracy both held at 100%).

This does mean the `top_k=5` baseline documented in Phase 1 (avg rank 1.8
across the four original test questions) is no longer what's running in
production; `top_k=8` is. Noted here rather than left silently
inconsistent between docs.

## 4. Resilience proving itself under a real failure

During one run, question 6 failed with a live Groq 429
(`tokens per minute` limit exceeded — the free tier's TPM cap, made easier
to hit by `top_k=8`'s larger prompts). The per-question `try/except` +
incremental-save pattern built in Phase 2 did exactly what it was built
for: the run didn't crash, the error was logged with its message, and the
other 19 results were saved to disk intact. This is the first time that
resilience fix was exercised by a real failure rather than a hypothetical
one, and it held. A `time.sleep(3)` was added between questions afterward
to reduce how often the TPM cap gets hit in the first place, now that
`top_k=8` prompts consume more tokens per call.

## 5. Generation failure: a one-character miss on the fixed abstain phrase

**Problem found:** on a later run, the out-of-scope question
"ما هي شروط الطلاق للضرر في القانون المصري؟" (divorce on grounds of harm)
was marked as a wrong answer, even though the model had retrieved nothing
relevant and cited nothing (`cited_articles: []`,
`hallucinated_citations: []`).

**Root cause:** the model's answer was
`"غير موجود في القانون المفهرد"` — one character off from the required
exact phrase `"غير موجود في القانون المفهرس"` (`د` instead of `س` in the
last word). The model had correctly recognized the question as
out-of-scope and attempted to abstain; it just mis-sampled the final
letter of the fixed phrase it was instructed to reproduce verbatim. Since
`ask.py`'s abstention check (`ABSTAIN_PHRASE in answer_text`) is
deliberately an exact substring match — chosen specifically to avoid fuzzy
text matching — a single-character generation slip was enough to register
as a false answer rather than a correct abstention.

**Fix:** lowered `temperature` in `call_groq()` from 0.1 to 0. The fixed
abstain phrase is meant to be copied verbatim, not generated with any
creative variance, so removing the main source of sampling randomness
targets the actual cause rather than loosening the detection logic itself.
Re-running the full 20-question set after the change produced a clean
result with no recurrence.

## 6. Final result

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Retrieval top-5 hit rate (in-scope) | 100% (15/15) |
| Citation accuracy (when retrieved) | 100% (15/15) |
| Hallucinated citations | 0 |
| False abstention (wrongly refused an answerable question) | 0% (0/15) |
| Correct abstention (out-of-scope) | 100% (5/5) |

20/20 questions correct on the final run. Both failures found along the
way were real, reproducible, and root-caused against actual output rather
than assumed away — one in retrieval (`top_k` too tight for a
lexically-mismatched question), one in generation (sampling variance on a
literal fixed phrase) — consistent with how every prior phase's failures
were investigated rather than just patched.
